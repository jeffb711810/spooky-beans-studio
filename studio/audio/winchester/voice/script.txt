This staircase leads straight into the ceiling.
This door opens onto a two-story drop.
And the woman who built them did it on purpose… to lose some ghosts.
It's 1881. Sarah Winchester has lost her baby… and now her husband.
She inherits the Winchester rifle fortune. About a thousand dollars… a day.
Then a psychic allegedly explains: the ghosts of everyone ever shot by a Winchester want revenge.
The cure? Build. And never stop.
So she buys a farmhouse in California, and the hammering starts. Day and night. For decades.
Most people remodel one kitchen and need a vacation. Sarah adds a hundred and sixty rooms.
Doors into walls. Windows in the floor. A staircase with seven turns that climbs nine feet.
And everything comes in thirteens. Thirteen bathrooms. A sink with thirteen drain holes. Even her will: thirteen parts.
Every midnight, she asks the spirits what to build next. Allegedly.
In 1906, an earthquake knocks off the top floors. She keeps building.
In 1922, Sarah dies. And for the first time in decades… the hammering stops.
The workers leave so fast, nails are still half hammered into the walls.
But historians never found that psychic. Maybe Sarah just really, really liked building.
Today, tourists get lost in her maze all the time.
Which… was kind of the point.
